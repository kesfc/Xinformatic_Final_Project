Xinformatics Final Project: Carbon Emissions Prediction System

About

Climate change poses a significant threat to many forms of life on Earth, including humans. Its effects are widespread, varied, and unpredictable, and it is caused primarily by anthropogenic industries. More explicitly, large-scale transportation and energy generation powered by fossil fuels releases exorbitant amounts of carbon dioxide—a greenhouse gas that traps heat at the Earth’s surface—into the atmosphere.

This study serves to examine the relationship between energy generation and carbon emissions in every country and the world throughout an era of transition from non-renewable energy to renewable energy. In a broadly studied field, it may not provide many insights more valuable than those from prior research, but it acts as an exercise in the application of informatics to climate science and a critical reminder of humanity’s inaction. Prior to observing the data, we hypothesized that carbon dioxide emissions would be shown to decrease and the ratio between renewable and non-renewable energy generated would be shown to increase throughout time in a majority of countries. Datasets on production of carbon dioxide and energy generation throughout time were collected and processed to remove irrelevant data. An additional dataset on populations was collected to normalize emissions data and reduce bias from population size and density across countries. A multiple linear regression model was trained on this data for each country as a predictor for carbon emissions given an energy production ratio and a year. Then, a website was developed to display the goal and results of the study, managing user input to present relevant plots.

The results indicated a negative correlation between emissions and time and between carbon emitted and proportion of energy generated by renewable sources in a significant number of countries. They also revealed a positive correlation between the proportion of energy generated by renewable sources and time in a greater number of countries. These trends imply that while renewable sources of energy are integrated worldwide, non-renewable sources of energy are, in some cases, not phased out.
